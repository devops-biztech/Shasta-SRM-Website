## Users

1. **Community and public** — Shasta County residents, local officials, media, and regional stakeholders forming an opinion of the plant. Their job: understand what SRM is, why burning wood waste for power is good for the forest and the grid, and trust that it's a responsible neighbor.
2. **Job seekers** — skilled trades and operators in the North State (plant operators, mechanics, equipment operators, laborers). Their job: see what roles are open, what they pay, what's required, and apply fast.

Secondary: residents and landscapers using the free wood waste drop-off program, and haulers/drivers who must review safety rules before arriving on site.

## Product Purpose

Shasta Sustainable Resource Management (SRM) runs one of Northern California's largest wood-fired biomass power plants in Anderson, CA. It turns forest thinning debris, mill residue, and community wood waste, material that would otherwise be pile-burned or left to fuel wildfire, into renewable baseload electricity.

The site exists to (a) make that sustainability and wildfire-resilience story vivid and credible to the public, and (b) recruit skilled workers. Success = community understands and supports the plant; qualified candidates apply.

## Positioning

A working, 24/7 renewable power plant that is also a forest-health tool: every megawatt is made from material removed to reduce wildfire risk in the Shasta-Trinity and Lassen National Forests and surrounding private lands. Unlike solar/wind, it's dispatchable baseload; unlike open pile burning, combustion is controlled and the energy is captured. Local, long-running (since 1987), and physically rooted in Shasta County.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Pages in scope: Home (story + impact), Our Story / How It Works, Sustainability & Wildfire, Careers (listings + apply), Wood Waste Drop-off (utility page: hours, accepted materials, directions, safety docs), Contact.
- Primary emphasis is community and job seekers; the drop-off and safety content is kept as a clearly reachable utility page but does not drive the homepage.
- Posted roles and wages (as of 2026-09): Mechanic I/II/III ($43.05 / $38.15 / $33.38/hr), Plant Operator ($42.38), Assistant Plant Operator ($35.15), Relief Assistant Plant Operator ($35.15), Equipment Operator I/II ($32.74 / $28.99), Plant Laborer ($22.71). All roles: zero-tolerance drug & alcohol policy.
- The **Wood to Energy Recycling Program** is the public drop-off. Its rules come from SRM's own handout (`public/docs/public-pile-handout.pdf`):
  - **Hours:** Mon–Sat 8 a.m.–5 p.m., closed Sunday. Hours are subject to change and posted at the gate. Program line: 530-339-7617, option 2.
  - **Accepted:** tree limbs and logs, chipped tree wood, pallets and crates, logging slash, clean scrap lumber, clean plywood, clean particle board.
  - **Rejected:** painted or treated wood, stumps, roots, **yard waste**, shrubs and bushes, bamboo, shakes and shingles, palm fronds, leaves, pine needles, grass clippings, cardboard, laminated wood, oleander, manzanita, juniper, crape myrtle, vines, dirt, soil, and rocks.
  - **Rules:** use the public gate (not the truck gate); an attendant inspects every load, and any load with unacceptable material is rejected; 5 MPH; stay 10 ft from other vehicles while unloading; no gas-powered equipment, no smoking, no climbing on vehicles, no tie-off pulls, and no unhitching trailers; sturdy footwear required; children under 14 stay in the vehicle; no pets; no salvaging.
- **Hours conflict to resolve with SRM:** the current website lists a "temporary delivery schedule" of Mon–Fri 5 a.m.–midnight and Sat 8–5, while the public handout says Mon–Sat 8–5. The working assumption is that the first is commercial fuel delivery and the second is the public drop-off.
- The **Safety Rules Book** (OHS-3300, 74 pp.) is the employee and contractor manual. Its acknowledgement form must be signed and returned within 10 days of receipt.
- No live generation data feed exists; any "live" output display is out of scope unless SRM provides a source.

## Product Principles

1. **Forest to grid, told concretely.** Every sustainability claim ties to a real mechanism or number: tons in, megawatts out, homes powered.
2. **Proud neighbor, not a corporation.** Local, human, rooted in Shasta County.
3. **Make applying effortless.** Pay, requirements, and the apply button are never more than one step away.
4. **Utility content stays findable.** Drop-off hours and safety docs are secondary, but anyone who needs them finds them in one click.
5. **Truth over polish.** Flag unverified numbers; never fabricate proof.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Audience includes older residents and field workers on phones; content must work on mobile with large tap targets and readable type. PDFs should get HTML equivalents where feasible.
